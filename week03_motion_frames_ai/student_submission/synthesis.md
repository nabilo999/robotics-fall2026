# Technical synthesis

A robot motion program can be technically correct but still fail in a real shared environment. For example, a robot may accurately follow its planned path and stop within the required distance, but a pedestrian may not expect it to turn suddenly or may not have enough space to pass. This can be a hazard to people using wheelchairs, people with limited mobility, or anyone carrying objects. The robot's code can calculate its position and velocity requirements while being an accessibility or safety problem. To evaluate the motion properly, additional evidence would be needed from real hallway trials testing stuff like minimum passing distance, stopping time, pedestrian reactions, and whether people can move around the robot comfortably. Workplace or building rules about robot operation would also need to be considered.

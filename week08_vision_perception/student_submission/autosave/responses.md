## mission_1.limitations
During practice, I experimented mainly with hue and saturation, although I also tried other settings, changing only one parameter at a time. My final hue range was 0–179, which allows every hue, and my saturation range was 83–255. The final mask included both mug and desk pixels, causing the selected box to include background. Because I changed several settings, I cannot confidently say that one parameter had more sway. A limitation is that a different cup with different colors might be excluded by these thresholds, while a background with similar colors could still be selected.

## line_following.comparison
In the straight practice run, the robot followed the line fairly steadily. The mask picked out the line, and the centering error stayed small, so it didn’t need much steering correction. On the curved track, the original settings still reached the finish, but the robot drifted more around bends and sometimes wobbled. I increased the gain from 0.08 to 0.16 and left everything else unchanged. On the second track run, I noticed less drifting, and the recorded error was lower overall. The stronger steering corrections kept the line closer to the middle of the camera view. It also finished sooner because it spent less time slowing down to handle large errors.

## line_following.limits
My practice run eventually entered LINE_LOST and stopped before I ended the controller. That means the image processing no longer found a usable line region, so the controller stopped instead of continuing without guidance. The recording alone doesn’t establish exactly why the line was lost. For a real delivery robot, following a colored line would not be enough to move safely around people. It would also need obstacle and distance sensing, such as depth cameras or lidar, and testing with people crossing its path, changing lighting, blocked routes and sensor failures. Reaching the end of this simulated track doesn’t prove it could handle those situations safely.

## final.synthesis
These missions showed me that detecting something in an image is not easy or reliable most the time. In normal_1 and dim_1, my classical detector selected too much of the desk instead of accurately locating the mug. The learned detector produced fewer false boxes in the conditions I compared, but it still missed mugs. In normal_1, its cup score was below my acceptance threshold, so it rejected the detection. This showed me how a setting meant to filter out uncertain predictions can also remove useful ones.
In my blue-only and four-color pillar attempts, the robot used the selected color’s position in the camera to decide which way to turn. Both attempts completed, although green and blue needed extra centering corrections. That showed the controller could use repeated camera feedback to correct its movement. It did not prove that the robot knew what the objects were or how far away they were.
In line following, both curved-track runs finished. After I increased the gain from 0.08 to 0.16, I observed less drifting, and the recording showed lower centering error and a shorter completion time. This supports the idea that stronger steering helped on this track, but it does not mean a higher gain would always be better. My practice run also entered LINE_LOST and stopped, showing why the robot needs to stop when usable visual evidence disappears. Overall, these results support the settings in these particular tests, but more varied scenes, distance sensing and obstacle checks would be needed for real-world use.
